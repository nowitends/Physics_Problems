# Task 04 – Energy and Momentum

## Problem Statement

A block of mass $m_1 = 0.5 \ \text{kg}$ slides down a frictionless track from a height $h = 3.0 \ \text{m}$. At the bottom, it collides and sticks to a second block of mass $m_2 = 1.5 \ \text{kg}$, which is initially at rest.

The objective is to determine the speed of the combined mass immediately after the collision.

---

## Theory

### Conversion of Potential Energy to Kinetic Energy

On a frictionless track, mechanical energy is conserved. The initial gravitational potential energy is fully converted into kinetic energy at the bottom.

$$
mgh = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
$$

This allows determination of the velocity just before collision.

---

### Conservation of Linear Momentum

During the collision, the blocks stick together, indicating a perfectly inelastic collision. Linear momentum is conserved:

$$
m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2) v_f
$$

Since the second block is initially at rest, $v_2 = 0$.

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Velocity of the first block before collision

Using energy conservation:

$$
m_1 g h = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2
$$

Canceling $m_1$ from both sides:

$$
g h = \frac{1}{2} v_1^2
$$

Solving for $v_1$:

$$
v_1^2 = 2 g h
$$

Substituting $g = 9.81 \ \text{m/s}^2$ and $h = 3.0 \ \text{m}$:

$$
v_1^2 = 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.0
$$

$$
v_1^2 = 58.86
$$

$$
v_1 = \sqrt{58.86}
$$

$$
v_1 \approx 7.67 \ \text{m/s}
$$

---

### Step 2: Apply conservation of momentum

Initial momentum:

$$
p_{\text{initial}} = m_1 v_1
$$

Final momentum:

$$
p_{\text{final}} = (m_1 + m_2) v_f
$$

Equating:

$$
m_1 v_1 = (m_1 + m_2) v_f
$$

Substituting values:

$$
0.5 \cdot 7.67 = (0.5 + 1.5) v_f
$$

$$
3.835 = 2.0 \cdot v_f
$$

Solving for $v_f$:

$$
v_f = \frac{3.835}{2.0}
$$

$$
v_f \approx 1.92 \ \text{m/s}
$$

---

## Final Result

$$
v_f \approx 1.92 \ \text{m/s}
$$

---

## Interpretation

The final speed is significantly lower than the initial speed of the first block due to the perfectly inelastic collision. In such collisions, kinetic energy is not conserved, and part of it is transformed into other forms such as heat and deformation. However, momentum remains conserved, resulting in a reduced velocity of the combined mass.

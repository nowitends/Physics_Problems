# Task 05 – Inelastic Collision

## Problem Statement

A runner of mass $m_1 = 70 \ \text{kg}$ moves with velocity $v_1 = 3 \ \text{m/s}$ and jumps onto a stationary cart of mass $m_2 = 140 \ \text{kg}$.

Determine the final velocity of the combined system.
Determine whether kinetic energy is conserved.

---

## Theory

### Conservation of Linear Momentum

For an isolated system:

$$
m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2) v_f
$$

For a stationary cart:

$$
v_2 = 0
$$

---

### Kinetic Energy in Inelastic Collisions

For a perfectly inelastic collision:

* Momentum is conserved
* Kinetic energy is not conserved

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Write known values

$$
m_1 = 70 \ \text{kg}
$$

$$
v_1 = 3 \ \text{m/s}
$$

$$
m_2 = 140 \ \text{kg}
$$

$$
v_2 = 0
$$

---

### Step 2: Apply conservation of momentum

$$
m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2) v_f
$$

Substitute values:

$$
70 \times 3 + 140 \times 0 = (70 + 140) v_f
$$

$$
210 + 0 = 210 v_f
$$

---

### Step 3: Solve for final velocity

$$
210 = 210 v_f
$$

$$
v_f = \frac{210}{210}
$$

$$
v_f = 1 \ \text{m/s}
$$

---

### Step 4: Initial kinetic energy

$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2
$$

$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2} \times 70 \times 3^2
$$

$$
K_i = 35 \times 9
$$

$$
K_i = 315 \ \text{J}
$$

---

### Step 5: Final kinetic energy

$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) v_f^2
$$

$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2} \times 210 \times 1^2
$$

$$
K_f = 105 \ \text{J}
$$

---

## Final Result

$$
v_f = 1 \ \text{m/s}
$$

Kinetic energy is not conserved.

---

## Interpretation

The system’s momentum remains constant because no external horizontal force acts on it. However, the kinetic energy decreases from $315 \ \text{J}$ to $105 \ \text{J}$.

This reduction occurs because the collision is perfectly inelastic. Part of the initial kinetic energy is converted into internal energy such as heat, sound, and deformation when the runner and cart move together after impact.

# Task 06 – Energy Dissipation

## Problem Statement

A tennis ball is dropped from a height of $2.0 \ \text{m}$. After each bounce, it loses $30%$ of its mechanical energy.

Determine the height reached after the second bounce.

---

## Theory

### Mechanical Energy and Height Relation

The mechanical energy of the ball at maximum height is purely gravitational potential energy:

$$
E = mgh
$$

Since $m$ and $g$ remain constant, energy is directly proportional to height:

$$
E \propto h
$$

Thus, a reduction in energy results in the same proportional reduction in height.

---

### Energy Loss per Bounce

After each bounce, the ball retains $70%$ of its energy:

$$
E_{\text{after}} = 0.7 , E_{\text{before}}
$$

This factor applies successively for each bounce.

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Initial height

$$
h_0 = 2.0 \ \text{m}
$$

---

### Step 2: Height after first bounce

Energy retained:

$$
E_1 = 0.7 , E_0
$$

Since $E \propto h$:

$$
h_1 = 0.7 , h_0
$$

$$
h_1 = 0.7 \times 2.0
$$

$$
h_1 = 1.4 \ \text{m}
$$

---

### Step 3: Height after second bounce

Again, $70%$ of energy is retained:

$$
h_2 = 0.7 , h_1
$$

$$
h_2 = 0.7 \times 1.4
$$

$$
h_2 = 0.98 \ \text{m}
$$

---

## Final Result

$$
h_2 = 0.98 \ \text{m}
$$

---

## Interpretation

The height after each bounce decreases because a portion of mechanical energy is lost during every impact with the ground. Since the height is directly proportional to mechanical energy, retaining $70%$ of energy results in the ball reaching $70%$ of the previous height after each bounce.

After two successive energy losses, the height reduces significantly from the initial $2.0 \ \text{m}$ to $0.98 \ \text{m}$, illustrating the cumulative effect of energy dissipation.

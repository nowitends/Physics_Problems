# Task 07 – Standing Wave Modes

## Problem Statement

A standing wave with four antinodes is formed on a string of length:

$$
L = 80 \text{ cm}
$$

Determine the wavelength of the standing wave.

---

## Theory

For a string fixed at both ends:

* The **number of antinodes** corresponds to the **harmonic number** $n$.
* The wavelength for the $n$-th harmonic is given by:

$$
\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

where:

* $L$ is the string length
* $n$ is the number of antinodes

This relation comes from the fact that each half-wavelength fits between two nodes (or between a node and an antinode at the string ends).

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Convert Length to Meters

$$
L = 80 \text{ cm} = 0.8 \text{ m}
$$

---

### Determine Wavelength

Given $n = 4$ antinodes:

$$
\lambda = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
\lambda = \frac{2 \cdot 0.8}{4}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{1.6}{4}
$$

$$
\lambda = 0.4 \text{ m}
$$

---

## Final Result

Wavelength of the standing wave:

$$
\lambda = 0.4 \text{ m}
$$

---

## Interpretation

* A standing wave with four antinodes corresponds to the **4th harmonic**.
* The wavelength is shorter than the string length, as multiple half-wavelengths fit along the string.
* In general, as the harmonic number increases, the wavelength decreases proportionally:

$$
\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

# Task 04 – Phase Difference

## Problem Statement

Determine the phase difference in radians between two points on a wave that are separated by a distance of

$$
\Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{3}
$$

where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the wave.

---

## Theory

The phase difference $\Delta \phi$ between two points separated by a distance $\Delta x$ along a wave is related to the wave number $k$ by:

$$
\Delta \phi = k . \Delta x
$$

The wave number $k$ is defined as:

$$
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
$$

Thus, the phase difference can be expressed as:

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} , \Delta x
$$

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

$$
\Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{3}
$$

Substitute into the phase difference formula:

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \cdot \frac{\lambda}{3}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{3} \text{ rad}
$$

---

## Final Result

Phase difference:

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{3} \text{ rad}
$$

---

## Interpretation

A separation of one-third of a wavelength corresponds to a phase difference of $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ radians.

This indicates that the two points are not in phase; the wave at one point leads the other by $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ radians in its oscillation cycle.

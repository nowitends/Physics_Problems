# Task 02 – String Harmonics

## Problem Statement

A guitar string has a length of $64 , \text{cm}$ and a fundamental frequency of $330 , \text{Hz}$.

Determine the speed of the wave on the string.

---

## Theory

For a string fixed at both ends, the fundamental mode (first harmonic) has one antinode and two nodes at the ends.

The wavelength of the fundamental mode is related to the string length by

$$
\lambda_1 = 2L
$$

The general wave relation is

$$
v = f \lambda
$$

Combining both relations for the fundamental frequency:

$$
v = f_1 \cdot 2L
$$

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Convert Length to SI Units

Given:

$$
L = 64 , \text{cm}
$$

Convert to meters:

$$
L = 0.64 \text{ m}
$$

---

### Determine Wavelength

For the fundamental mode:

$$
\lambda_1 = 2L
$$

Substitute:

$$
\lambda_1 = 2 \times 0.64
$$

$$
\lambda_1 = 1.28 \text{ m}
$$

---

### Calculate Wave Speed

Using the wave equation:

$$
v = f \lambda
$$

Substitute:

$$
v = 330 \times 1.28
$$

$$
v = 422.4 \text{ m/s}
$$

---

## Final Result

Wave speed on the string:

$$
v = 422.4 \text{ m/s}
$$

---

## Interpretation

The wave speed depends on the physical properties of the string, such as tension and mass per unit length. A relatively high speed indicates that the string is under significant tension, allowing waves to propagate quickly along it.

The fundamental mode produces the lowest possible frequency, and all higher harmonics would correspond to shorter wavelengths and higher frequencies.

# Task 06 – Wave Equation

## Problem Statement

A wave is described by:

$$
y(x,t) = 0.05 \sin(2\pi x - 50\pi t)
$$

where $x$ and $y$ are in meters and $t$ is in seconds.

Determine:

a) Amplitude $A$

b) Wavelength $\lambda$

c) Frequency $f$

d) Wave speed $v$

---

## Theory

A general sinusoidal wave traveling in the positive $x$-direction is expressed as:

$$
y(x,t) = A \sin(kx - \omega t)
$$

where:

* $A$ is the **amplitude**
* $k$ is the **wave number**, $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$
* $\omega$ is the **angular frequency**, $\omega = 2\pi f$
* $f$ is the **frequency**
* $\lambda$ is the **wavelength**
* Wave speed is related by $v = f \lambda = \frac{\omega}{k}$

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### a) Amplitude

The amplitude is the coefficient in front of the sine function:

$$
A = 0.05 \text{ m}
$$

---

### b) Wavelength

The wave number is:

$$
k = 2\pi , \text{(from equation)}
$$

Relation between wave number and wavelength:

$$
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \implies \lambda = \frac{2\pi}{k}
$$

Substitute $k = 2\pi$:

$$
\lambda = \frac{2\pi}{2\pi} = 1 \text{ m}
$$

---

### c) Frequency

The angular frequency from the equation is:

$$
\omega = 50\pi
$$

Relation between angular frequency and frequency:

$$
\omega = 2\pi f \implies f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

Substitute $\omega = 50\pi$:

$$
f = \frac{50\pi}{2\pi} = 25 \text{ Hz}
$$

---

### d) Wave Speed

Wave speed is related to frequency and wavelength:

$$
v = f \lambda
$$

Substitute:

$$
v = 25 \cdot 1
$$

$$
v = 25 \text{ m/s}
$$



## Final Result

a) Amplitude:

$$
A = 0.05 \text{ m}
$$

b) Wavelength:

$$
\lambda = 1 \text{ m}
$$

c) Frequency:

$$
f = 25 \text{ Hz}
$$

d) Wave speed:

$$
v = 25 \text{ m/s}
$$

---

## Interpretation

* The **amplitude** represents the maximum displacement of the wave.
* The **wavelength** is the distance between successive peaks.
* The **frequency** indicates how many oscillations occur per second.
* The **wave speed** shows how fast the wave propagates along the medium.

All quantities are directly obtained from the wave equation using standard wave relationships.

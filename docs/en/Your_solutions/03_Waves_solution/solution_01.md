# Task 01 – Wave Properties

## Problem Statement

A sound wave in air has a frequency of $440  \text{Hz}$. The speed of sound in air is $343  \text{m/s}$.

Determine the wavelength of the sound wave in air.

Then determine its wavelength in water, where the speed of sound is $1482  \text{m/s}$.

---

## Theory

The fundamental relationship between wave speed, frequency, and wavelength is

$$
v = f \lambda
$$

where
$v$ is the wave speed,
$f$ is the frequency,
$\lambda$ is the wavelength.

Rearranging to solve for wavelength:

$$
\lambda = \frac{v}{f}
$$

For a given wave, the frequency remains constant when transitioning between media. Only the speed and wavelength change.

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Wavelength in Air

Given:

$$
v_{\text{air}} = 343  \text{m/s}, \quad f = 440  \text{Hz}
$$

Using the wavelength formula:

$$
\lambda_{\text{air}} = \frac{v_{\text{air}}}{f}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
\lambda_{\text{air}} = \frac{343}{440}
$$

$$
\lambda_{\text{air}} = 0.7795 \text{ m}
$$

---

### Wavelength in Water

Given:

$$
v_{\text{water}} = 1482  \text{m/s}, \quad f = 440  \text{Hz}
$$

Using the same relation:

$$
\lambda_{\text{water}} = \frac{v_{\text{water}}}{f}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
\lambda_{\text{water}} = \frac{1482}{440}
$$

$$
\lambda_{\text{water}} = 3.368 \text{ m}
$$

---

## Final Result

Wavelength in air:

$$
\lambda_{\text{air}} = 0.78 \text{ m}
$$

Wavelength in water:

$$
\lambda_{\text{water}} = 3.37 \text{ m}
$$

---

## Interpretation

The wavelength in water is significantly larger than in air because the speed of sound is much higher in water. Since frequency remains constant across media, the increase in wave speed leads directly to an increase in wavelength.

This demonstrates that wavelength is directly proportional to wave speed when frequency is fixed.

# Task 03 – Electrostatic Equilibrium Between Two Charges

## Problem Statement

Two fixed charges $q_1 = +4 , \text{C}$ and $q_2 = +9 , \text{C}$ are separated by a distance of $2 , \text{m}$. A  charge $q_3 = +1 , \text{C}$ is placed somewhere on the line between them.

Determine the position where $q_3$ is in electrostatic equilibrium.

---

## Theory

### Condition for Electrostatic Equilibrium

A charge is in equilibrium when the net electric force acting on it is zero:

$$
F_{\text{net}} = 0
$$

For two charges acting on a third charge along a straight line:

* Forces must be equal in magnitude
* Forces must act in opposite directions

---

### Coulomb’s Law

The force between two point charges is

$$
F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}
$$

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Define Positions

Let the distance between $q_1$ and $q_2$ be $2 , \text{m}$.

Assume:

* $q_1$ is at $x = 0$
* $q_2$ is at $x = 2$
* $q_3$ is placed at a distance $x$ from $q_1$

Thus:

* Distance between $q_1$ and $q_3$ is $x$
* Distance between $q_2$ and $q_3$ is $(2 - x)$

---

### Step 2: Direction of Forces

Since all charges are positive:

* $q_1$ repels $q_3$ to the right
* $q_2$ repels $q_3$ to the left

Thus, forces are opposite in direction.

---

### Step 3: Apply Equilibrium Condition

Set magnitudes equal:

$$
k \frac{q_1 q_3}{x^2} = k \frac{q_2 q_3}{(2 - x)^2}
$$

Cancel common terms $k$ and $q_3$:

$$
\frac{q_1}{x^2} = \frac{q_2}{(2 - x)^2}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
\frac{4}{x^2} = \frac{9}{(2 - x)^2}
$$

---

### Step 4: Solve the Equation

Take square root on both sides:

$$
\frac{2}{x} = \frac{3}{2 - x}
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
2(2 - x) = 3x
$$

$$
4 - 2x = 3x
$$

$$
4 = 5x
$$

$$
x = \frac{4}{5} = 0.8 , \text{m}
$$

---

## Final Result

The equilibrium position is

$$
x = 0.8 , \text{m from } q_1
$$

or equivalently

$$
1.2 , \text{m from } q_2
$$

---

## Interpretation

The equilibrium point lies closer to the smaller charge ($q_1 = 4 , \text{C}$).

This occurs because:

* The larger charge ($q_2 = 9 , \text{C}$) exerts a stronger force
* A greater distance is required to reduce its influence
* Balance is achieved when both forces become equal in magnitude

Thus, electrostatic equilibrium between unequal charges always occurs closer to the smaller charge.
